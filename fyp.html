Final-year research project

P300 EEG-BCI for
vision-guided robotic object retrieval.

A camera scene is converted into a visual oddball interface. P300 responses are decoded by a Transformer, accumulated into an object decision, then checked against the camera before robotic execution.

View the public code

System pipeline

Brain signal to object action.

Camera scene→Four-object oddball→EMOTIV FLEX EEG→Transformer→Evidence score→Robot command

P300 protocol and acquisition

A flash-aligned EEG decision process.

The frozen camera scene supplies four candidate objects. Each candidate is flashed nine times, producing 36 flashes in one burst. The interface uses a 100 ms flash duration with a 1.0 s stimulus-onset interval.

EMOTIV FLEX records 32 EEG channels at 128 Hz. The preprocessing path uses 0.1 to 20 Hz fourth-order zero-phase filtering, common-average reference, and epochs spanning -0.2 s to +0.8 s. Each epoch is 129 × 32 samples.

Transformer decoding

Channel-aware P300 interpretation.

The deployed model uses gated channel projection, a 192-dimensional representation, three Transformer encoder layers, four attention heads per layer, 1024-dimensional feed-forward blocks, GELU activation, 0.20 dropout and two target/non-target logits.

It scores each candidate through the mean raw target-minus-nontarget logit across its nine flashes. The highest evidence score becomes the selected object.

Vision and robotics

Stable selection before motion.

After the EEG decision, the selected candidate is revalidated against the frozen camera scene. Camera-derived object identity and localisation are prepared for an inverse-kinematics pick-and-drop preview before the robotic arm command is issued.

Research contribution

This work brings visual stimulation, LSL EEG acquisition, P300 decoding, evidence accumulation, camera validation and robot orchestration into one operating pipeline.
