Final-year project · EEG-BCI

P300 EEG decoding for vision-guided robotic object retrieval.

In my final-year project the camera scene is turned into a visual oddball interface with four objects. The EEG responses to the flashes are decoded in one Transformer batch and accumulated into an object decision, the decision is checked against the camera frame once more before a pick-and-drop action is prepared for the arm.

Design

System architecture

The scene, the flash protocol, the EEG processing and the arm control form one chain. Each object keeps the same identity from the camera frame until the arm moves.

Signal and decision design

Evidence is accumulated before the arm moves

Frozen four-object scene→Target flashes→32-channel EEG segments→Transformer logits→Object margin accumulation→Revalidation and motion preview
4objects in each frozen visual scene
36flash-aligned epochs, nine repetitions per object
129 × 32time samples by EEG channels per epoch

Visual protocol

Four-object stimulus interface

The camera identifies exactly four candidate objects. Each object keeps a fixed identity through the whole flash sequence, hence the classifier result stays attached to the correct physical target.

P300 decoder

Transformer-based evidence scoring

The deployed route applies 0.1–20 Hz zero-phase filtering and a common-average reference. A gated channel projection feeds a 192-dimensional Transformer with three encoder layers and four attention heads, the feed-forward blocks are 1024-dimensional with GELU and 0.20 dropout. The final object is the one with the highest mean target-minus-non-target logit across its nine flashes.
